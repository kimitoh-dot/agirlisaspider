a window (actually a mirror) you decorated this room to express who you were. the room was built on Facebook's infrastructure. when Facebook shut Pet Society down in 2013, the rooms disappeared. everything you made inside a platform belongs to the platform.
🚪
a door that leads somewhere we thought the early internet was a place you could move through. it had topology. it had rooms and corridors. you could go places. the current internet is a feed. it doesn't have rooms. it has one very long wall.
🏷️
a landlord notice (pinned to the wall) "learning that the thing you loved had a landlord." the internet felt free because it was so new the landlord hadn't noticed yet. then they noticed.
📦
a box of things that don't load anymore the object persists. the place it connected you to does not. you carry the Tamagotchi. the town it belonged to is a 404. you carry the memories. the server is gone.

Wayback Machine this page has been saved. you were here.

this website is a web in the literal sense. every image here, including the background, was found through the Wayback Machine, retrieved using keywords and hashtags that describe the places i'm writing about. the images are indexed. the methodology is the archive.

i grew up on the early internet. not browsing it, but living in it. Club Penguin, Tamatown, Tumblr, WeHeartIt. places that, according to Aranda, Wood and Vidokle, never existed. i disagree; my girlhood was shaped by being present in them, and this web is a counter-testimony.

i'm writing about these places in the style of Georges Perec — from memory, without reference images, unedited. trying to remember a place the way you remember a room you loved: by its details, rather than its facts. the hashtags are how i index what i find. they are also the residue of a culture that learned to categorise itself into types.

in I'm Like a PDF But a Girl, Freider quotes Lacan: writing [...] is a mode of becoming-spider. by writing these places into existence through Perec's methodology, i am becoming spider. by collecting and weaving images from the Wayback Machine, i am web-weaving. the web you are looking at is the artefact.
